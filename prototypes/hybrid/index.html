    Case studies

    Real problems from real teams, written as problem, approach and outcome.

    4 hrs → 10 min

    E2E automation from zero for an Electron desktop app

    Critical-path validation on Master Tour Desktop, once fully manual.

    problem

    Every release candidate was regression tested by hand, with heavy setup, and the team was moving toward faster releases.

    approach

    Chose Playwright through a written PRD, sequenced tests by business value, and designed the suite to run serially because parallel workers collided on the app's local SQLite database. Wired it into Bitbucket Pipelines on every PR and weekly.

    outcome

    Release regression went from manual to automated, and critical-path validation dropped from about 4 hours to about 10 minutes.

    10 → 2 hrs/wk

    Cutting QA's support load with structured office hours

    Ad hoc support pings were eating a third of QA's week.

    problem

    QA was absorbing roughly 10 unplanned hours a week of tier 2/3 support, cutting into coverage work.

    approach

    Set up a standing office-hours block, started at twice a week, measured again, then stepped down to once a week once the numbers held.

    outcome

    About 8 hours a week went back to planned coverage, and the sessions surfaced 2 critical bugs straight to engineering.

    20+ cases

    An AI-gated bugfix-to-automation pipeline

    Every bugfix now leaves a traceable test behind it.

    problem

    Bugs were getting fixed with no reliable way to make sure an automated case followed.

    approach

    Two gates before a bugfix PR opens: Claude checks the change against existing unit coverage, then drafts the follow-up QA story into a time-boxed intake epic linked from the PR.

    outcome

    20+ automation cases across 10+ fixed bugs in the first three months, without anyone having to remember to ask.

    0 → 1

    Standing up a product's quality function from nothing

    The order of operations I follow on any product.

    problem

    As Eventric's first dedicated QA hire there was no process to build on and everything felt urgent at once.

    approach

    Assessment first, then manual gates before automation, foundations before features, and critical path before edge cases, expanding outward by risk tier.

    outcome

    The sequence held up solo and again with a second QA engineer. Team size changed the pace, not the order.
